Customer analytics · real data

Who are the best customers, who is leaving, and what are they worth?

A London online retailer of gifts and homewares (UCI Online Retail II, Dec 2009 to Dec 2011, 1.07 million invoice lines). Every number below comes from SQL that is versioned, tested and validated against what actually happened next.

Product revenue net of cancellations, excluding postage, fees and discounts. Customers = people with at least one purchase.

Overview

Revenue peaks every September to November

Product sales by month. About 13% comes from invoices without a customer ID: real revenue, but it cannot be attributed to anyone, so it stays out of the customer analyses.

Question 1 · RFM segmentation

A quarter of the customers bring 69% of attributable revenue

Recency, frequency and monetary scores from 1 to 5 (ties always get the same score), mapped to ten segments on the R × F grid. Each segment comes with an action for the CRM team.

Question 2 · Churn

A simple rule, validated back in time, and where it has no signal

A customer has churned after more than X days without buying. To choose X honestly, the rule was applied at a past cut-off using only data available then, and compared with who actually came back in the next six months.

Cut-off 10 Jun 2011

Cut-off 10 Dec 2010 (right after the peak)

Who really did not come back, by days without buying at the cut-off

Customers 91–180 days without buying did not come back about as often as the average customer (46% against 48% in June 2011): just past the threshold, the rule says little. It separates the active from those already gone. That is why the end of the data splits "at risk" (91–365 days) from "inactive" (over a year).

Question 3 · Retention cohorts

About one in five customers buys again the next month

Share of each cohort (month of first purchase) that bought again N months later. The first month of the data (Dec 2009) is left out: those customers are not new, just the first record we have. Early 2010 cohorts are still mixed with returning customers (left censoring), so comparing years is inconclusive.

Question 4 · Customer lifetime value

The forecast beats a seasonal naive one on all three measures

Predicted 6-month value = probability of staying active (by segment, same season one year earlier) × average order value × purchases per month (Gamma-Poisson smoothed). Made on 10 Jun 2011 and checked against the real revenue to 10 Dec 2011. The total is close partly because errors offset: by segment the bias goes from −17% (Champions) to +199%.

Top-20% capture: revenue captured by the 20% of customers with the highest prediction, relative to a perfect top 20%.

How it was built
UCI · SHA-256→ PostgreSQL bruto→ limpo (CHECKs)→ analise (SQL)→ dq (18 checks)→ Power BI · notebook · this page

Cleaning, RFM, churn, cohorts and CLV are SQL migrations (Alembic) tested with pytest, including hostile cases. A data-quality schema checks that totals reconcile across layers before any number is published.
